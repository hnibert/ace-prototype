Meaghan Bryan started her career in marine science surveying coral reef associated populations in the Florida Keys. She went on to earn a Master of Science degree from North Carolina State University where she studied estuarine connectivity followed by a PhD from the University of British Columbia in population dynamics and fisheries stock assessment modeling. As part of her degree, she developed a multispecies stock assessment model to derive species-specific stock status determinations for bottom fish and conducted a management strategy evaluation to explore auxiliary data sources to improve the management of a data-limited, multispecies fishery.

Meaghan joined NOAA Fisheries as a Research Fishery Biologist in 2011 and has conducted fisheries stock assessments for data-limited species in the US Caribbean, more data-rich grouper species in the Gulf of America (formerly the Gulf of Mexico), and flatfishes in the Bering Sea, Aleutian Islands, and the Gulf of Alaska. Her research interests include advancing data-limited assessment models, multispecies modeling, and incorporating multispecies and environmental considerations into stock assessments and fisheries management. Meaghan is located at the Alaska Fisheries Science Center in Seattle and has been a co-lead of the Gulf of Alaska Climate Integrated Modeling (GOACLIM) project with Carey McGilliard since 2023.
