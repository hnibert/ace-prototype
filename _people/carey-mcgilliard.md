Carey is a quantitative ecologist at the Alaska Fisheries Science Center with expertise in fisheries stock assessment, management strategy evaluation, and spatial aspects of population dynamics. She earned a PhD from the School of Aquatic and Fishery Sciences at the University of Washington, where she studied spatial stock assessment modeling and used management strategy evaluation to develop and test novel harvest control rules for use in data limited fisheries.

At the Alaska Fisheries Science Center, Carey led the development of a spatial management strategy evaluation to develop and investigate bycatch control rules under consideration by the North Pacific Fisheries Management Council. She has conducted numerous production stock assessments for groundfish in the Gulf of Alaska, Bering Sea, and Aleutian Islands, focusing on modeling environmental influences on fish populations.

She spent a decade as the science advisor to the United States delegation of decision-makers to the International Pacific Halibut Commission. In that role, she served as co-chair of the IPHC’s Management Strategy Advisory Board, facilitating discussions about fisheries objectives and harvest strategies for Pacific halibut between scientists, user groups, tribes, and managers from the US and Canada.

Carey joined GOACLIM as co-lead-PI with Meaghan Bryan in 2023 and is currently working on how to synthesize the results from climate-integrated ecosystem models in the Gulf of Alaska.
