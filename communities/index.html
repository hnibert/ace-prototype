Community research
brings local priorities into the science

These projects support community-led questions around food security, species and habitat change, and accessible tools for planning in a changing environment. The work is designed to help residents, managers, and researchers share information and make decisions with stronger local context.

Subsistence calendars
& food security research

Community food security work highlights the seasonal rhythms that shape harvesting, sharing, and preparation. These projects connect subsistence knowledge with data that can support planning and communication across communities.

Species of concern
bring together local observations

Species of concern bring together local observations, ecological information, and community priorities around animals that may be vulnerable or especially important to subsistence practices.

Learn more about this informal designation used by wildlife agencies to show declining numbers which lack data to warrant formal listing.

Community-tailored
species distribution modeling applications

Species distribution modeling helps researchers examine how environmental change may affect where species are found in the future. These tools can be adapted for community planning by putting local questions and regional knowledge into the analysis.
