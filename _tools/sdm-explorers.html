What are Species Distribution Models?

Species distribution models (SDMs) link environmental information with data on how common (and how abundant) different species are to discern the types of environments each species is associated with under contemporary conditions. Then, scientists use projections of future environmental conditions from climate models to estimate where the conditions that a species is common and abundant are likely to be in the future.

Predictive Modeling

Scientists project using different possible scenarios, which represent alternative possible pathways for global greenhouse gas emissions. SDMs use historical relationships of distribution and environmental conditions to predict distributions, but environmental conditions don't explain all of the variability in where we observe organisms, so other conditions can impact where fish or crab species actually occur.

Regional Distributions

SDMs are used to project changes in the distributions of several groundfish and crabs in the Bering Sea, including walleye pollock, Pacific cod, snow crab, red king crab, and others. Browse these distribution shifts in relation to coastal communities and fishing ports using the search below, or check out the results in more detail using the ACLIM2 SDMs explorer.
