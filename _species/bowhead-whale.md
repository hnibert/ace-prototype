#Justification for Classification (use a '>-' block scalar indicator for text below)
justification: |
    Yupik whalers of St. Lawrence Island, Barrow, Kaktovik, and Wainwright hold Tradtional Knowledge that supports observations of abundance, migration patterns, seasonal movements, and distribution (Noongwook et al., 2007; Huntington & Quakenbush, 2009; Quakenbush et al., 2010). 
    
    Through Tradtional Knowledge, we understand that when surfacing to breathe during a hunt, the animal is considered giving itself to the hunter, prevoking deep cultural connections to this species for Traditional hunters and Natives. 
    
    "This phenomenon is known asangyi (from the Yupik root ang-, which refers to giving something") (Noongwook, et al., 2007).  More broadly, highly-productive hunters commonly distribute marine mammal products to other households through non-commercial networks of sharing and trade, which is key to consider in Alaska's mixed economy.
