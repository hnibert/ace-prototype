#Justification for Classification (use a '>-' block scalar indicator for text below)
justification: |
    Chum salmon were selcted as a species of concern due to their cultural significance throughout many rural Alaskan communities including those along the lower Yukon River Drainage network. Once a species in abundance, Chum salmon have faced drastic declines in the Eastern Bering Sea Region with escapment goals not being met in recent years. Chum salmon carry significant cultural significance that contributes to physical, spiritual, and mental well-being within the region bringing people together and connecting people to their heritage. 
    
    In addition, this species is tied to greater ecosystem health as it plays a role within the greater system. The decline of this species raises great food security concerns within the region. Concerns over bycatch of this species exists in commercial groundfish/pollock fisheries, and many subsistence closures have occurred in recent years leading to the need to focus on this species in the face of climate change, uncertainty, and continued commercial pelagic trawling practices. And in 2021, commercial landings of chum salmon totaled 63.7 million pounds and were valued at $56.6 million, according to the NOAA Fisheries commercial fishing landings database making them of significant value to commercial fishers as well <https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/chum-salmon>.

#Cultural Significance (use a '>-' block scalar indicator for text below)
cultural_significance: |
    An important source of spiritual and physical sustenance for Alaska natives. Salmon provides a significant source of nutrition in an environment where a subsistence way of life is a necessity for remote communities who do not have the same access to store-bought foods as other communities do. In this way, salmon are critical to the food security of these communities (Loring & Gerlach, 2010).
    
    While salmon is harvested for subsistence, it is shared locally and widely both within and between communities in a way which strengthens social bonds (Trainor et al. 2021). 
    
    The sharing of salmon can also help support the adaptive capacity of certain communities in the face of social and ecological changes (Scaggs et al. 2021). 
    
    Beyond this, salmon has important cultural importance to many Indigenous groups as it. Human-salmon relationships are central to their way of life and contribute to their spiritual, psychological, and emotional wellbeing (Carothers et al. 2021; Amberson et al. 2016).
