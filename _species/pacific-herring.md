#Justification for Classification (use a '>-' block scalar indicator for text below)
justification: |
    Pacific Herring were selected as a species of concern due to the central role they play in marine food webs and their significance as a commercial and subsistence species in many Alaskan communities. Herring have supported some of Alaska’s oldest commercial fisheries, and subsistence fisheries for herring in Alaska predate recorded history. 
    
    Alaska Natives harvest Pacific Herring for cooking and smoking the meat, rendering oil from the flesh, and the harvest of eggs for subsistence use as food, and there exists both Local Knowledge and Tradtional Knowledge that supports the historical ecology of this species (Thornton, et al., 2010)  
    
    And lastly, in 2021, 74,470,099 pounds were harvested in Alaska with an ex-vessel value of $9,257,290, making this species of significant value to commercial fishers in the region today (Alaska Department of Fish and Game, 2021). 
    
    A previous, large commercial fishery existed in Norton Sound, however, after overharvest from international commercial fleets, the fishery was deplinished. Yet, LK from personal communication with community members in Nome and Unalakleet support the returning abundance of these populations in Norton SOund, which are now only harvested in bait fisheries (Reynolds, 2023)
