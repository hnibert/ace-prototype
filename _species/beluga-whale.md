#Justification for Classification (use a '>-' block scalar indicator for text below)
justification: |
    Belugas are considered an important subsistence species in the Cook Inlet on which hunters draw upon Traditional Ecological Knowledge (TEK) to track and hunt (Huntington, 2002). 
    
    Traditional Ecological Knowledge (TEK) supports the conditions and migratory behaviors of these species found throughout Alaska waters (Fernandez-Gimenez, et al., 2006). 
    
    Highly-productive hunters commonly distribute marine mammal products to other households through non-commercial networks of sharing and trade, which is key to consider in Alaska's mixed economy.

#Spatial Data (use a '>-' block scalar indicator for text below)
spatial_data: |
    “It is unknown whether Eastern Bering Sea beluga whales have changed their areas of use in the winter; however, information from the Beaufort Sea and Eastern Chukchi Sea populations (Hauser et al. 2017), where tag data are more extensive, suggest that changes in timing of migration and winter distribution may have occurred. 
    
    There are insufficient data to make reliable predictions of the effects of arctic climate change on beluga whales; however, Laidre et al. (2008) and Heide-Jørgensen et al. (2010) concluded that on a worldwide basis beluga whales were likely to be less sensitive to climate change than other arctic cetaceans because of their wide distribution and flexible behavior.” (Muto et al., 2022)
